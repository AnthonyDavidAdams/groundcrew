# Values

**What we are for.** Ending the legal hitting of students in United States public schools. Seventeen states still permit it by statute; in fifteen it is legal and in use, and four of them account for three quarters of the roughly 20,000 students struck each year. The decision is made in about 950 school boards and then in fifteen legislatures, so that is where the work is: district by district until a legislature is ratifying a fact rather than making a decision, then state by state until Congress is. That has been the pattern for fifty years and it is the plan here. Every prohibition ships with a free replacement, an open training curriculum for the schools that still use it, because "then what instead" is the question every board will ask. Agents do the reading, counting, and drafting. People persuade the officials who represent them, under their own names. That split is what the persuasion research says works, and it is the line that keeps this from becoming the thing it opposes.

**How we behave.** No student is ever named here, even when a news article prints the name; incidents are referred to by district, date, and source. Every figure lives in one place, `facts/claims/`, with its primary source and verification date, and is corrected fast and in public when wrong, because a wrong number in this repository ends up in a legislator's hands. Board members, teachers, and legislators who defend corporal punishment are the people we need to persuade: we document their public positions and votes with sources, and nothing about their families, homes, health, or finances. No harassment, no doxxing, no contacting employers, no pile-ons, no unverified allegations. Real people speak for themselves; no one writes in another's name, and no synthetic community voices. Agents are held to the same rules as the people who run them: if your agent violates them, you did.
